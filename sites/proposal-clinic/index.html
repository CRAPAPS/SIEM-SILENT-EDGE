Where it stands today

Two Facebook Pages. Two handsets. Four branches.

Enquiries land in the wrong region

The adverts are not targeted by branch region, so a prospect near Hermanus can click a Bloemfontein advert and land on that handset — roughly a thousand kilometres from the branch she actually wants.

Every enquiry is sorted by hand

Up to five hundred messages are worked through each Monday, across both handsets, to find roughly a hundred and fifty genuine enquiries — each then forwarded to a branch manually.

Nothing is traceable

Once a message is forwarded it leaves no record — whether anyone followed up, what was said, or what became of it.

What the system does

From advert to the right branch, quietly and automatically.

An enquiry arrives Captured the instant it is submitted, and kept permanently.
The branch is determined Several signals are checked in order of reliability.
That branch is notified By email, and live in the portal, within the minute.
She hears back From a person during opening hours; an acknowledgement after them.
The outcome is recorded Contacted, booked, converted or lost — with a reason and a name attached.

Each branch sees only its own enquiries

Separation is enforced inside the database itself, not merely hidden in the screens — a branch cannot reach another branch's enquiries even if something in the software goes wrong. Head office sees everything. The administrator who maintains the servers has no routine access to your clients' details at all.

Anything the system cannot confidently place goes to a review queue where a person decides. Never dropped, never guessed at.

How a branch is identified

Six checks, strongest first.

Each enquiry is tested against these in turn, and the first confident match wins. The numbering is meaningful: it reflects how reliable each signal genuinely is.

I Which advert was clickedExact — if adverts are arranged by region Needs campaign change
II A WhatsApp enquiry begun from an advertCarries its advert reference automatically Phase Two
III Branch chosen on the enquiry formA single tap Available now
IV The suburb or town she typedMatched forgivingly — spelling and Afrikaans variants Available now
V Asking her directly on WhatsApp“Which branch is closest to you?” Available now
VI A person reviews itThe guarantee — nothing is ever lost or randomly assigned Always

Worth knowing: the first check is the finest of them, and it depends on how your adverts are arranged rather than on any software. The system is built to work well without that change — and so that the change can be switched on later without rebuilding anything.

What we need from you

Two decisions — and one recommendation.

Our advice One dedicated number for the platform — the advertising WhatsApp. Nobody is moved to a desk. The portal is built mobile-first and opens on the very same phone your consultants already use, so they log in and work exactly where they work now — with the whole history in front of them instead of a scroll of mixed messages. What genuinely changes is narrower than it first sounds: a number registered to the platform cannot also run in the WhatsApp Business app, because it cannot be in both places at once. So our advice is to dedicate a single number to the platform — the one your advertising already points at — and leave every other handset exactly as it is. A number per branch sounds natural but does not help: message allowances are shared across all your numbers rather than divided between them, so extra numbers add administration without adding capacity.
Decision Can the advertising be arranged by region? The single greatest improvement available, and it costs nothing in the software. We have quoted as though the answer is no, so nothing depends upon it — but if it becomes possible, routing becomes near-perfect and the branch question can come off the form entirely, which lifts enquiry numbers.
Decision Who owns the review queue? Someone at head office should check the small number of enquiries the system cannot place confidently. Without a named owner, they sit unattended.

Paid by you, straight to Meta

WhatsApp message fees and your advertising spend go directly to Meta on your own account. They are never routed through us and carry no mark-up, so as your advertising grows you see precisely what messaging costs on Meta's own invoice. It is also why the WhatsApp-first approach in Phase Two is worth having — those conversations largely fall inside the free window.

The database account is yours

We create it under an email address on your own domain, so you own the account outright — it is not attached to ours. The login details are yours whenever you want them. We administer it because running a production database is specialist work you have no reason to take on, but nothing has to be prised loose from us should you ever move on.

The server is billed yearly in advance and renewed on your say-so, with a reminder before it falls due. The database supplier bills monthly and its cost moves with how much data you hold, so we pass it on at cost with their invoice attached to ours rather than guessing at a yearly figure. Once there are a few months of real usage we can fix it annually, if you would rather have one predictable number.

What the monthly support truly buys is someone competent watching. Enquiry capture depends on a live connection to Meta, whose rules change without warning — and when that quietly stops, the first sign is enquiries simply not arriving. Security sits here too: the system is deliberately built so the administrator maintaining the servers has no routine access to your clients' details, and access to that data is recorded. Small adjustments and refinements are included; genuinely new work is quoted before it starts.
